# Founder-Market Fit Analysis

## Framework used

Investors don't buy ideas; they buy **unfair advantages in the specific person**. We score founder-market fit across six vectors, each 1–10.

| Vector | What it proves | Score | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lived pain** | You have personally lost money/time to this problem | 9/10 | Geoffrey has been on both sides: a professional navigating LinkedIn/Mercor/Upwork with a non-trivial global relocation dimension, and a builder who has *used* the AI-talent platforms as both candidate and buyer of labour. |
| **Domain fluency** | You can name the 20 things outsiders get wrong | 8/10 | Knows the economics of Mercor/micro1/Turing (hourly rate bands, evaluation funnels, payout cycles), the recruiter workflow, and the EU relocation/visa stack. |
| **Technical ability to ship alone** | You can build the MVP without a team | 9/10 | Flutter + Node/TS + Postgres is exactly the stack; CV parsing, embeddings, ranking and affiliate tracking are all solo-feasible in 2026. |
| **Distribution instinct** | You can get the first 1,000 users without a budget | 6/10 | **Weakest vector.** No existing audience of 50k+. This is the #1 risk and must be attacked first (see GTM). |
| **Market timing insight** | You understand why this is possible *now* and not in 2019 | 8/10 | AI-talent platforms created rate transparency; LLMs made the data layer cheap; ghost jobs broke trust. |
| **Network in the wedge** | You can get 10 design partners this month | 7/10 | Access to global AI-talent communities (Mercor/micro1/Turing discords, Flutter & DS communities, relocation groups). |

**Composite: 7.8/10** — strong on insight + build, thin on distribution. This is a *buildable, unproven-distribution* founder, which is a normal and acceptable pre-seed profile **only if** the first 90 days prove distribution.

## The three genuine unfair advantages

1. **You are the customer.** Every hypothesis can be tested on yourself in an hour. Most competitors (VC-backed, well-funded, US-based) are building for personas they interview quarterly.
2. **You've seen the whole stack from the inside.** You know that Mercor's real moat is its evaluation data, that Turing's is its talent ops, that Upwork's is liquidity — and that *none* of them owns the candidate's decision. You know the seams.
3. **You can ship the decision layer solo.** The hard technical work here (entity resolution across 15 sources, scoring, embedding-based matching) is now a one-person job. In 2020 it was a 6-person team.

## The three genuine gaps

1. **No audience.** CAC will be the constraint. Mitigation: build in public + programmatic SEO + a referral engine where the *product itself* is the growth loop (each scored opportunity that the user wins becomes shareable proof).
2. **No proprietary data on day one.** Career Twin needs outcome data. Mitigation: seed it with *self-reported* outcomes from week one (a 15-second "did you apply / did you get it?" loop) and treat every answer as gold.
3. **Single point of failure.** If the founder stops, so does the company. Mitigation: ruthless simplicity (no marketplace, no social) so the operational surface stays tiny; document everything.

## Founder-market fit verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can this founder credibly build the MVP alone in 30–60 days? | **Yes.** The stack matches; the scope is deliberately small. |
| Does this founder have insight competitors lack? | **Yes** — the decision layer vs. listing layer distinction, and the AI-talent-platform rate transparency. |
| Is the founder's distribution weakness fatal? | **Only if unaddressed.** It is addressable with programmatic SEO + build-in-public + a referral loop. But it must be the *first* thing tested, before more code is written. |
| Should this founder raise first or validate first? | **Validate first.** Raising now means selling a deck; raising at €10k MRR means selling a machine. Same effort, 10x better terms. |
