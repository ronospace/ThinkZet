# GRO-01 – the first 20 users

Everything needed to run the first cohort: who to invite, the message, the test steps, and the questions to ask. No page
changes are required for this run.

## 1. Who to invite

Twenty people, five in each bucket, so the feedback covers more than one kind of user.

| Bucket | Who | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| Underpaid | People who suspect they are below market | The rate check lands hardest here |
| Job hunting | Anyone actively applying now | Tests the radar and the tracker |
| Relocating | People moving country or considering it | Tests the market comparison |
| Freelance or contract | Consultants and contractors | Tests the hourly rate path |

Avoid friends who will be kind. The first twenty exist to find what breaks.

## 2. The invite message

Short, one ask, no pitch deck.

> Hi [name] – I built something and I need twenty honest testers, not polite ones.
>
> It reads live job postings and tells you what your profile is actually worth, then which opportunities are worth your
> time. One minute, no CV upload, no account needed to see the number.
>
> https://thinkzet.com
>
> If the number looks wrong, tell me. That is the useful part.

For WhatsApp or Telegram, drop the greeting and keep the last line. For LinkedIn, remove the link and send it after they
reply, because a first message with a link reads as spam.

## 3. Test steps

Send these only after they have seen their rate. Five steps, under three minutes.

1. Open https://thinkzet.com and run the rate check with your real role, years and country.
2. Read the number and the confidence beside it. Does it match what you would expect, and by how much is it off?
3. Sign in with Google or an email code, then let it build your radar.
4. Open one opportunity and mark it Applied.
5. Rate the radar with the five buttons at the foot of the list, and press Share if you are willing to.

## 4. Feedback questions

Five questions, asked in one message, answerable in a single reply.

1. What number did it give you, and what did you expect?
2. Was the confidence label clear, or did it need explaining?
3. Which opportunity did you open first, and why that one?
4. What would you have to see to pay 19.99 a month?
5. What is the one thing that felt wrong or unfinished?

Question 4 is the only one that matters commercially. Question 5 is the only one that finds bugs.

## 5. What to record

For each of the twenty, record four facts, so the cohort produces numbers rather than anecdotes.

| Field | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Rate shown and expected rate | Answers to question 1 |
| Signed in, yes or no | Analytics funnel, `auth.verified` |
| Applied marked, yes or no | Tracker state |
| Would pay, yes, no or maybe | Answer to question 4 |

Target after twenty: at least 12 sign-ins, at least 6 Applied marks, at least 3 definite yes on price. Below those, the
problem is the product, not the message.

## 6. Timing

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Send five invites, one per bucket |
| 2 | Send the next five, after reading the first replies |
| 3 | Send the last ten only if the first ten produced at least one yes on price |
| 7 | Review the four recorded facts across all twenty and decide the next cohort |

Sending all twenty at once wastes the cohort: the first five tell you what to fix before the other fifteen see it.
