# Exit Scenarios

## 1. The honest framing

Exit planning at pre-seed is mostly a discipline: it forces you to ask **"who would ever want this, and what would they be buying?"** Every scenario below is ranked by probability and by whether the *build decisions* change. Most don't — but one does (see §6).

**Base case is not an exit.** The base case is a profitable, independent, €10–30M ARR software company. That is a good outcome and the most likely one.

## 3. Scenario detail

### 3.1 Strategic acquisition — AI talent marketplace (20%, most likely real exit)

**Why they'd buy:**
- They are in a **supply war**. Their biggest cost is candidate acquisition and their biggest weakness is candidate loyalty.
- CareerOS owns the **decision moment** — precisely the moment a contractor chooses which platform to work on.
- The **outcome dataset** (person → opportunity → outcome across platforms) is the single most valuable asset in this category and they structurally cannot build it.
- Buying us converts a neutral referral partner into a proprietary funnel, overnight.

**Why they'd pay €40–120M:** at €8–15M ARR growing 100%+ with 88% margins, that's 5–8x ARR — standard for a strategic supply acquisition. The strategic value (candidate funnel + dataset) often justifies more.

**What kills this exit:** the marketplace fails (Mercor/micro1 valuations compress); or our referral revenue makes us look like a competitor rather than a neutral partner.

---

### 3.2 Strategic acquisition — job boards / careers (10%)

**Why they'd buy:** Indeed and LinkedIn cannot build the neutral decision layer, but they can buy it and run it as a premium candidate-funded tier (exactly as LinkedIn did with Lynda/Learning, Indeed with Glassdoor's salary data). CareerOS = the "intelligence tier" they can't create internally.

**Anti-trust/conflict risk:** the buyer may have to keep us operationally independent (a "CareerOS, a LinkedIn company" model) — which is fine for us.

**Valuation driver:** ARR multiple + strategic defensive value.

---

### 3.3 HR-tech / data acquisition (8%)

**Why they'd buy:** our **skills graph + comp benchmarks + outcome data** is directly monetisable inside ATS and HRIS products (comp planning, skills taxonomy, market pricing). Workday/Personio/HiBob all buy data. This is the highest-multiple path per euro of revenue because it's a data asset, not a consumer app.

**Trigger:** the Comp-Data API reaching 50+ paying B2B customers.

---

### 3.4 Financial services (4%, highest upside)

**Why they'd buy:** the Opportunity Rail — offer advances, income smoothing for contractors, payroll for global talent. Anyone with a balance sheet and a talent relationship wants the decision moment. Wise, Deel, DailyPay, Earnin, Chime all have this thesis.

**Valuation:** infrastructure multiples (8–15x revenue, or strategic).

**Risk:** regulated, capital-intensive. Only credible if the Talent Graph is real.

---

### 3.5 PE roll-up (12%)

A €5–15M ARR, 30–40% net margin, 88%-gross-margin SaaS business is a textbook PE asset. At 3–6x ARR this is a €40–90M outcome requiring no heroic growth — the "boring good outcome."

---

### 3.6 Stay independent (15% + 2% IPO)

Honestly the most attractive for a solo founder. €10–30M ARR, 35% net margin, 6–10 people, no board. €4–10M/yr of owner earnings. Many founders would take this over a €100M exit with 2x liquidation preference.

---

### 3.7 Acqui-hire / soft landing (15%)

If the product doesn't find PMF but the *team and the dataset* are valuable, a €3–12M acqui-hire is realistic. This is not a failure scenario; it's a floor.

---

### 3.8 Shutdown (14%)

Base-rate honesty: most seed-stage companies die. This is 14% *conditional on* the Phase 0–2 gates being met. If the gates fail, this rises to 60%+.

## 6. The one build decision that changes based on exit thinking

> **Whether to build the employer/recruiter side.**

- **If we want the HR-tech data exit:** build it early (Year 2), because the buyer wants a B2B revenue line.
- **If we want the marketplace exit or the independent path:** **never** build it, because it destroys the neutrality moat that makes us valuable to *both* candidates and acquirers.

**Verdict:** don't build it. The neutrality position is worth more than the B2B revenue it would add, and it preserves every option. Revisit only at Year 3 with the Talent Graph as a *data* product (licensed), not a *sales* product.

## 7. Exit verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the most likely outcome? | A €40–120M strategic acquisition by a talent marketplace or careers platform in Year 3–4, **or** a profitable independent company. |
| What is the most valuable outcome per unit of effort? | The independent/PE path: €5–15M ARR with 6–10 people is achievable and keeps 100% of the upside. |
| What must never happen? | Becoming an employer-side sales company in Year 1–2. That path leads to a €10M acqui-hire, not a €100M exit. |
| Is exit planning changing the plan? | Only in one place (no employer side). Everything else stays the same: **own the decision layer, capture outcomes, stay neutral.** |
