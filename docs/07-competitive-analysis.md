# Competitive Analysis

## 1. The strategic frame

Every competitor answers one of three questions:

| Question | Who answers it | Business model |
|---|---|---|
| "What opportunities exist?" | LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, RemoteOK, Otta, Wellfound, FlexJobs | Listings + ads |
| "Give me work / give me workers" | Mercor, micro1, Turing, Upwork, Fiverr, Braintrust, Contra, Hired | Take rate |
| **"What should I do next, and why?"** | **Nobody** | — |

**The structural reason none of them can follow us:** every one of them monetises its *own* inventory. A platform cannot credibly tell you "this fellowship is better for you than my job listing." Neutrality is our product and their structural impossibility.

## 2. Competitor-by-competitor

### 2.1 LinkedIn

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Key strengths** | The only true professional identity graph (1B+). Network effects on both sides. Recruiter seat revenue is enormous and durable. Owns the professional *social* layer. |
| **Key weaknesses** | Engagement-optimised, not outcome-optimised. Feed is noise. Job search is keyword-based, not decision-based. Massive ghost-job and spam problem. Application tracking is weak. Never tells you "don't apply." |
| **Market position** | Default professional infrastructure. Category king of listings + social. |
| **Revenue model** | Recruiter seats, ads, Premium, Learning, Sales Navigator. |
| **Defensible advantages** | Identity graph, recruiter relationships, habit, data volume. |
| **Blind spots** | Cannot recommend opportunities outside LinkedIn. Cannot be honest about odds (advertisers + employers are the customer). Structurally incapable of cross-platform comparison. |
| **Gaps Karyvo exploits** | Neutrality across all 15 opportunity types; probability scoring; outcome tracking; non-social, decision-first UX. **We are not competing with LinkedIn — we consume it.** |

---

### 2.2 Mercor

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Key strengths** | Interview-at-scale AI ("Mercor interviews") creates a proprietary evaluation dataset — their real moat. Excellent match quality for AI-lab customers. High rates, global talent supply. |
| **Key weaknesses** | Single-sided supply: you're a worker in *their* marketplace, not an owner of your own career. No cross-platform view. No career development. Opaque tiering. Platform risk: your income depends on their pipeline. |
| **Market position** | Leading AI-talent marketplace for labs and AI companies. Strong brand in the AI contractor community. |
| **Revenue model** | Take rate / margin on placements (typically 15–30%+). |
| **Defensible advantages** | Evaluation data + enterprise customer relationships + brand with labs. |
| **Blind spots** | Zero incentive to tell a user "you'd earn more at micro1" or "a fellowship is better for you." No long-term career relationship — the relationship ends when the contract ends. |
| **Gaps Karyvo exploits** | **Rate comparison across Mercor/micro1/Turing/direct** is a killer feature. Career Twin continuity across platforms. Referral revenue from Mercor *as a partner*, not a competitor. |

### 2.3 micro1

### 2.4 Turing

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Key strengths** | Enormous global developer supply funnel; strong ops and vetting; US-enterprise relationships; long operating history. |
| **Key weaknesses** | Reputation for low-ball rates in some geographies; heavy process; the candidate is a resource in a pipeline. |
| **Market position** | Scale player in remote developer staffing. |
| **Revenue model** | Margin on staffed developers. |
| **Defensible advantages** | Supply volume, vetting ops, enterprise sales. |
| **Blind spots** | Candidate trust. Turing's incentive is to place you at the lowest acceptable rate. It can never show you that. |
| **Gaps Karyvo exploits** | **Rate transparency as a trust product.** "Turing offered €X; the market for your profile is €X+30%." This is a wedge with a real emotional payload. |

### 2.7 Braintrust

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Key strengths** | User-owned network model (token/ownership narrative) was genuinely novel; low take rate (0% talent / 10% client) attracted top freelancers. |
| **Key weaknesses** | Token model lost momentum; liquidity never reached escape velocity; UX dated; brand confusion post-crypto winter. |
| **Market position** | Declining mid-tier freelance marketplace. |
| **Revenue model** | 10% client fee. |
| **Defensible advantages** | Community goodwill, low-fee positioning. |
| **Blind spots** | Liquidity solved with incentives instead of utility. |
| **Gaps Karyvo exploits** | **Lesson: don't build a marketplace first.** Build the decision layer, monetise, then consider liquidity. |

### 2.8 Upwork

## 4. Overcrowded vs. underserved — final verdict

### 🔴 Do not enter (overcrowded, winner-take-all, or commoditised)
- Generic job aggregation & search (Indeed/LinkedIn own it)
- Employer-side AI sourcing (200+ funded startups)
- AI interview/assessment SaaS (regulated, crowded, enterprise sales)
- CV builders (free substitutes)
- Professional social networking (network effects locked)
- Freelance marketplace liquidity (Upwork/Fiverr own it; Braintrust is the cautionary tale)

### 🟢 Enter here (underserved, defensible, monetisable)
- **Cross-type opportunity decisioning** ← core
- **Realistic probability-of-success modelling** ← differentiation
- **Rate & salary intelligence for AI/contract work** ← emotional wedge
- **Visa-sponsorship as a first-class, reliable filter** ← high-intent niche
- **Individual-side outcome tracking** ← data moat seed
- **Non-job opportunities for professionals** (fellowships, grants, accelerators) ← blue ocean
- **Non-US-centric career intelligence** ← geographic whitespace

## 5. The three "copy threats" and how we survive them

| Threat | Likelihood | Why it's survivable |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn adds an AI career assistant** | High | It cannot be neutral across competitors, cannot recommend non-LinkedIn opportunities, and its ad model forbids honest odds. It will be a feature, not a product. |
| **A funded startup copies the UX** | Medium-High | UX is copyable; the **outcome data and Career Twin history** are not. Speed + data compounding is the defence. |
| **A talent marketplace builds it to defend supply** | Medium | Mercor/micro1 could add "career planning," but only for their own inventory — which is exactly the limitation users hate. They'd be building a worse version of us. |

**The real competitive risk isn't a competitor. It's that nobody cares enough to pay.** That is why validation precedes everything.
